ER to Relational Mapping
This is the "bridge" moment in DBMS.
You've spent time drawing diagrams (ER), and now it's time to turn those drawings into actual Tables (Relational Schema).

Think of the ER Diagram as the blueprint of a house and the Relational Mapping as the actual construction.

What is ER to Relational Mapping?

ER to Relational Mapping is the formal process of converting entities, attributes, and relationships into a set of tables (relations).
In this stage, we move from conceptual bubbles and diamonds to rows and columns.

The "Golden Rules" of Mapping
To make this transition simple for students, we follow a set of standard rules:

Entities become Tables: 
Every strong entity type becomes a separate table.

Attributes become Columns:
The properties of the entity (Name, Age, ID) become the headers of the table.

Primary Keys stay Primary:
The unique identifier you chose in the ER diagram becomes the Primary Key (PK) of the table.

Relationships become Foreign Keys:
This is the most important part—how tables "talk" to each other is determined by where we place the Foreign Keys (FK).

Why is this step so critical?
Without proper mapping, a great design can fail in the implementation phase. Mapping ensures:

Data Integrity: 
Relationships are strictly enforced using keys.

No Data Loss:
Every piece of information in the diagram finds a home in a table.

Structure for SQL:
Once the mapping is done, writing the CREATE TABLE statements in SQL becomes a simple "copy-paste" job.
