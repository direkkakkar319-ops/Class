Entity-Relationship (ER) modeling streamlines database design by using three key concepts:
Generalization
Specialization
Aggregation.

Generalization
Generalization simplifies by extracting common attributes from 
multiple entities and creating a higher-level entity.
This is a bottom-up approach.

Example:-
For instance, in a university model, "Faculty," and "Admin.Staff" entities might share attributes like "E_Id" and ""E_Name." A generalized "Employee" entity would hold these shared traits.

Specialization
Specialization refines by creating sub-entities from a generalized entity.
Each sub-entity gains unique attributes. This is a Top-Down approach.

Example:-
In our university example, the Employee entity can be specialized as "Faculty," and "Admin.Staff".
Essentially this is the opposite of Generalization.

Aggregation
Aggregation treats a relationship between entities as an entity itself. It's used when relationships have their attributes.

Example:-
In ER models, aggregation simplifies complex scenarios. Consider a "Student" working on a "Project" that requires specific "Guide". To connect this, the "WORKS_ON" relationship between "Student" and "Project" aggregates into an "Assignment" entity. Then, a "REQUIRE" relationship links "Assignment" and "Guide" entities, representing the needed project guide.
